## Definition and intuition

Retrieval metrics compare ranked results with declared relevance judgments. Precision, recall, AP/MAP, reciprocal rank, and nDCG emphasize different properties, so their cutoff, denominator, relevance unit, and absent-query policy must be explicit.

Read this lesson as a sequence: identify the input, follow the transformation, inspect the result, and explain a failure. The worked example and recorded lab below make those boundaries visible.

## How it works

### Precision and recall at k

Precision@k counts relevant returned documents among the first k and divides by k; unfilled positions count against this convention. Recall@k divides retrieved relevant documents by all judged relevant documents. The atlas first collapses overlapping chunks to unique document IDs. Binary relevance means grade greater than zero, while the QA support set is stricter than topical relevance.

### Average precision and mean average precision

AP@k adds precision at each relevant rank up to k, then divides by every relevant document in the judgment set, including relevant documents below k or missing from the returned list. This is not the alternative min(R,k) normalization. MAP is the mean of defined per-query AP values. L12 excludes no-relevant-document queries from MAP and reports the 14-of-16 denominator; it evaluates their withholding behavior separately rather than disguising undefined AP as a perfect score.

### Discount and first-hit metrics

nDCG uses graded gains 2^grade − 1 discounted by log2(rank + 1), then divides by an ideal ordering at the same cutoff. MRR uses only the first relevant rank, so it misses improvements to later relevant results. Neither a high ranking metric nor a topically related passage proves that a generated answer is correct. Preserve per-query results and inspect which relevance judgment changed a score.

### Mean average precision

For Q queries with defined AP, MAP = sum of AP values / Q. Each included query has equal weight, regardless of how many relevant documents it has. The reported L12 comparison uses 14 answerable evaluation queries; its two absent-answer queries remain in the collection and are evaluated separately. A different absent-query policy or AP denominator changes the metric and must be stated before comparing numbers.

## Worked example

With order A,B,C,D and relevance grades 2,0,1,2, there are three relevant documents. At k=3, precision is 2/3. AP contributions are 1 and 2/3, giving AP@3 = (1 + 2/3)/3 = 5/9. The omitted relevant D remains in the denominator. Move C ahead of B and recompute; then compare nDCG, which also rewards higher grades.

## Implement it

From the repository root, run `python3 scripts/run-examples.py --id e6-retrieval-metrics --offline` after installing the foundation environment. This standard-library example calculates the stated mechanism or replays hash-linked actual model evidence from the published snapshot. It does not perform a fresh neural-model inference. {/* example:e6-retrieval-metrics:start */}

```python
import sys
sys.path.insert(0, 'examples/foundations/src')
from atlas_foundations.e6.retrieval import metrics
score = metrics(['A','B','C','D'],{'A':2,'B':0,'C':1,'D':2},3)
assert abs(score['ap']-5/9)<1e-12
print('AP terms:',score['ap_terms'],'all relevant:',score['relevant_documents'])
print('P@3:',round(score['precision'],4),'AP@3:',round(score['ap'],4),'nDCG@3:',round(score['ndcg'],4))
print('Absent-query AP:',metrics(['A'],{},3)['ap'])
```

## When to use it

Use a versioned judged query set to compare retrieval changes on identical cases. Select thresholds and parameters on design/validation queries and preserve evaluation questions for the final reported comparison.

## Limits and common mistakes

Judgments may be subjective, incomplete, or stale. L12’s closed fictional corpus makes the labeling convention inspectable; it does not establish real-world relevance quality. Queries with no relevant documents require an explicit policy, and scores from different relevance units cannot be compared casually.
