## Definition and intuition

Contrastive training learns representations from relationships between examples, encouraging selected positives to score above selected alternatives. The choice of positives, negatives, score, and loss defines what similarity the model learns.

Two support messages use different words for the same issue. A useful representation should bring that pair together while distinguishing messages about other issues. Incorrectly chosen pairs can teach exactly the wrong relationship.

## How it works

In a softmax contrastive objective, an anchor scores a positive and negatives; the loss penalizes a low normalized probability for the positive. Temperature controls the sharpness of this comparison. In binary negative-sampling objectives, each pair is scored independently through logistic losses. These are related ideas, not identical formulas.

Parameters are updated through the chosen loss. Hardcoded vectors can make a hand calculation visible, but they are not learned representations. The playlist workflow supplies actual positive co-occurrences, sampled negatives, and optimizer updates to embedding tables.

Negative selection is a modeling assumption. Another relevant item can be sampled as a false negative; duplicates or train/test overlap can inflate results. A large batch can supply more comparisons while also increasing these risks and compute needs. Evaluate on the task relationship you intend to preserve.

### The important distinction

A negative can be unlabeled rather than truly irrelevant.

## Worked example

For positive score 2 and negative score 0 at temperature 1, softmax gives the positive probability about 0.881 and loss about 0.127. At temperature 0.5, the same score gap is amplified. The scores in this trace are invented, so no model-quality improvement is implied.

## Implement it

Run the small mechanism from the repository root with `python3 scripts/run-examples.py --id foundation-contrastive-training`. Its values are illustrative; it isolates the stated operation rather than pretending to load a trained model.

{/* example:foundation-contrastive-training:start */}

```python
from math import exp, log

positive, negative, temperature = 2.0, 0.0, 1.0
p = exp(positive / temperature) / (
    exp(positive / temperature) + exp(negative / temperature)
)
print("positive probability:", round(p, 3), "loss:", round(-log(p), 3))
```

## When to use it

Use contrastive objectives when pair relationships are available or can be defined responsibly, such as retrieval pairs or item co-occurrences. Inspect alternative definitions of a positive and compare rankings on held-out examples before adding training complexity.

## Limits and common mistakes

A negative can be unlabeled rather than truly irrelevant. A successful retrieval of the training example itself can be trivial. Similarity under one objective may reflect style or popularity more than the semantic relationship your product needs.
