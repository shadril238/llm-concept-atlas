## Definition and intuition

A Transformer-based Sequential Denoising Auto-Encoder (TSDAE) learns sentence representations by encoding a damaged sentence and reconstructing the original with a decoder. The deployed artifact is the encoder; reconstruction is a training surrogate for a useful sentence representation.

Follow the input, objective, update, and evaluation as separate steps. The worked example and experiment below connect the explanation to an inspectable implementation.

## How it works

### Corrupt only the permitted corpus

The lab uses training-only service requests and seeded 60% whitespace-word deletion, retaining at least one word. The original sentence is the target, not a class label. Fixed corruptions make this small experiment inspectable; they do not reproduce the full stochastic scale of the paper. Validation/test sentences are excluded from updates.

### A sentence bottleneck conditions reconstruction

The actual DenoisingAutoEncoderLoss uses CLS pooling and sends a [batch,1,128] vector to decoder cross-attention. The decoder sees gold previous tokens during teacher-forced training and predicts the next original token. Padding targets are ignored with the decoder pad ID in this pinned loss. Encoder/decoder compatible parameters are tied; new decoder cross-attention is initialized separately. With only one context key, its attention softmax is always one: query/key selection gradients can be zero while value/output projections still learn. The run checks this distinction rather than asserting every cross-attention tensor must receive a nonzero gradient.

### Inspect task quality beyond reconstruction

CLS is a pooling choice used by the book/paper experiment, not proof that contextual mean pooling has no positional information. A lower reconstruction loss can reflect better token prediction without better retrieval geometry. L17 records both curves, selects among trained checkpoints using validation retrieval MRR, and checks actual encoder and decoder gradients, tied parameters and reload equivalence.

## Worked example

A request such as “How do I book a workshop room?” may lose most words under deletion. Compare its saved damaged input with the actual greedy reconstruction. The small decoder produces repetition in the author run. Several curve intervals lower reconstruction loss without improving validation MRR; test retrieval also fell from the CLS baseline. ## When to use it

Use unsupervised reconstruction as a candidate adaptation stage when domain text is available but relationship labels are scarce. Test the resulting encoder on the actual downstream task.

## Limits and common mistakes

Teacher-forced token predictions are easier than free reconstruction because they see correct earlier tokens. A decoder that models common wording well may still discard distinctions important for search. Reconstruction performance is not a general sentence-embedding benchmark.

Author execution checks tensor/mask contracts, actual parameter updates, split provenance and artifact reload. It does not constitute independent lesson acceptance. Keep training quality, task quality, runtime and generalization claims separate.
