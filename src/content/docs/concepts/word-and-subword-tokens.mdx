## Definition and intuition

Word tokenization treats words as units; subword tokenization composes text from vocabulary pieces that may cover full words or smaller segments. Character and byte tokenization use finer units with different coverage and length tradeoffs.

A product name never seen during tokenizer training arrives in a support message. A whole-word vocabulary may map it to unknown, while a subword vocabulary can often compose it from known pieces. That composition may use more positions.

## How it works

BPE learns a sequence of merges of frequent adjacent units. Encoding applies the learned merge rules; the merges are not chosen anew using every user’s input statistics. WordPiece uses a learned vocabulary and its own segmentation procedure. Unigram scores alternative segmentations with a probabilistic vocabulary model.

The algorithm alone does not determine the output. Training corpus, vocabulary budget, normalization, pre-tokenization, and special-token settings also matter. Two BPE vocabularies can segment identical text differently.

A character in a writing system is not necessarily one byte or one token. UTF-8 encodes many characters as multiple bytes, and a byte vocabulary’s visible piece notation may look unusual. Use full-sequence decoding and offsets to interpret the actual tokenizer rather than guessing from English spaces.

### The important distinction

Do not label slicing a visible prefix as subword tokenization.

## When to use it

Choose a tokenizer through the paired model contract. To study compactness, compare identical inputs with fixed revisions and report special markers separately. For a new tokenizer design, evaluate the target corpus and downstream task rather than selecting only the lowest count.

## Limits and common mistakes

Do not label slicing a visible prefix as subword tokenization. A word split into three pieces needs aligned annotation labels; copying a B-entity tag to every piece can create false entity starts.

## Check your understanding

<details><summary>Does a shorter encoding establish that the tokenizer’s paired model is better at the task?</summary><p>No. It establishes a length difference for that input under that encoding. The model’s training objective, data, architecture, and task quality require separate evaluation. A compact representation can still remove a distinction the task needs.</p></details>

<details><summary>Why does “cats” sometimes appear as “cat” and “##s”, while an emoji behaves differently?</summary><p>Those are vocabulary-specific WordPiece units. Emoji coverage and byte behavior depend on the tokenizer; inspect actual IDs and full decoding instead of applying the English continuation notation universally.</p></details>
