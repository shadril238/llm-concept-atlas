## Definition and intuition

Sentiment analysis assigns an attitude label to a stated target under an explicit annotation policy. Positive and negative are a task-specific choice; mixed, neutral, sarcastic, and uncertain text require deliberate handling.

A review can praise the acting while condemning the plot. A useful classifier needs a decision policy before it needs a model: are we predicting the overall opinion of the film, sentiment toward an actor, or a specific aspect? Those are different targets. A forced binary decision cannot represent every reasonable interpretation.

## How it works

### The annotation contract

L07 uses 0 = negative and 1 = positive for a licensed subset of UCI movie-review sentences. UCI deliberately selected positive/negative examples; that does not make neutral inputs disappear in a real application. Twelve original challenge probes add negation, mixed opinions, sarcasm, and software/product language. Their gold labels are author judgments, especially on ambiguous cases, so the browser reports them separately from the 80 UCI test sentences.

### Scores and evidence

A classifier computes scores conditioned on its training and label set. A large positive softmax value does not establish what the writer intended, and it does not prove that the input is in domain. Keep the original text and inspect false positives as well as false negatives. A label is a prediction about this defined task, not a factual explanation of a customer.

### Negation and target scope

A word-count baseline can treat “not good” like “good”. Contextual models can represent the negation, but still make mistakes on long clauses or sarcasm. “Not a masterpiece, but good entertainment” is not equivalent to “not good entertainment”. The position of a negation and the clause expressing the final judgment both matter.

## Worked example

Consider “The actors are wonderful, but the story is so incoherent that I regret watching it.” Our original probe assigns overall film sentiment 0. The positive word wonderful does not determine that label. If the task were acting quality, a positive label could be correct. Preserve the text, target, and policy together so an apparent model error can be distinguished from an annotation mismatch.

## Implement it

```python
text = "not good"
positive_words = {"good", "excellent", "wonderful"}
baseline = int(bool(set(text.split()) & positive_words))
gold = 0
print("keyword prediction:", baseline, "policy label:", gold)
assert baseline != gold  # a deliberately exposed baseline failure
```

## When to use it

Use sentiment analysis for an evaluated review-analysis tool or aggregate feedback exploration. For routing billing versus bugs, define an intent taxonomy and train/evaluate that task; sentiment labels are not interchangeable with request types.

## Limits and common mistakes

An English binary model can confidently misread unfamiliar domains or languages. Sentence-level splits also do not prove independence at the reviewer or source-review level when those identifiers are absent. Inspect mixed examples and sampling limits before making product claims.
