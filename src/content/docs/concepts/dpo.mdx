## Definition and intuition

DPO adapts a language-model policy using preferred and rejected completions for the same prompt. It compares their sequence log-probability gap under the policy with the same gap under a frozen reference, then applies a logistic preference loss. It does not require the separately trained scalar reward model.

## Theory: how it works

### Completion log probabilities

For each candidate y and prompt x, sum log pi(y_t | x,y_before_t) over completion tokens. L23 includes real EOS, excludes prompt and padding, and shifts causal labels once. The policy and reference receive identical token IDs and masks. They must not score a different template or silently truncate different parts of the pair. Sum log probabilities differ from mean token probabilities and from a product computed directly in floating point.

Let pc/pr be the policy chosen/rejected log probabilities and rc/rr the reference values. The relative margin is (pc−pr)−(rc−rr). Original sigmoid DPO loss is −log sigmoid(beta×margin). Stable log-sigmoid avoids underflow. Beta is a preference-strength/drift-related objective parameter, not the optimizer learning rate; changing either has a different effect.

At identical initial policy and reference, the relative margin is zero and loss is log2. The loss still has a nonzero gradient through policy probabilities; freezing both models would prevent learning. L23 checks exact initial agreement in evaluation mode, fixes reference parameters and verifies its hash after training. The policy uses a fresh zero-update LoRA adapter on the selected merged SFT checkpoint. Adapter dropout is zero here, keeping initial comparison deterministic.

### Preference data templating

Keep one prompt and two separate completions. Do not paste chosen and rejected into a single assistant response or let one candidate's continuation become the other's prompt. The same EOS policy applies to each. Our maximum256 tokens is enforced by rejection, and test contexts remain outside fitting/selection. Chosen/rejected labels are rule-authored transformations of the attributed Dolly subset; label preference is relative and independent review remains pending.

### The important distinction

A positive relative margin says the policy has shifted toward the chosen completion relative to the reference. It need not mean the chosen candidate has higher raw likelihood: both sequences can have very low probability, and summed log probabilities carry length effects. L23 reports raw preference accuracy, relative accuracy and ties separately, plus independent task metrics on actual base/SFT/DPO generations. A decrease in validation DPO loss is not proof of better grounded answers.

The explicit torch loop follows the original sigmoid formulation documented by pinned TRL0.23.1; it does not claim to execute DPOTrainer or every trainer variant. There is no online generation, reward-model query, length normalization, label smoothing or reference refresh inside this training objective.

## Worked example

Suppose policy log probabilities are pc=−3 and pr=−5 while reference values are rc=−4 and rr=−5. Policy gap2 exceeds reference gap1 by 1. With beta 0.1, loss is about 0.6444, below log2. Adding a common constant to either pair changes neither gap. If pc and pr are per-token means instead of sequence sums, this would be a different objective.

The actual run begins from L21's saved float32 merged SFT model. Forty optimizer steps, batch 2, LR0.001 and beta 0.1 are explicit. Every ten steps, validation pair loss selects among trained checkpoints; heldout prompts are not used to tune hyperparameters. The adapter is saved and loaded against the same selected SFT identity before scores are checked again.

## Implement it

This standalone Python 3 calculation uses only the standard library. It does not download or train a model. The [complete generation labs](/llm-concept-atlas/labs/generation-adaptation/) provide the pinned environments, actual training entrypoints, staged notebooks, raw results and artifact checks.

{/* example:e8-dpo:start */}

```python
from math import exp, log1p

pc, pr, rc, rr, beta = -3.0, -5.0, -4.0, -5.0, 0.1
margin = (pc - pr) - (rc - rr)
z = beta * margin
loss = max(0, -z) + log1p(exp(-abs(z)))
print("reference-relative margin:", margin)
print("DPO loss:", round(loss, 4))
print("No separate scalar reward model appears in this calculation.")
```

## When to use it

Use DPO when you have trustworthy pairwise preferences and a suitable SFT starting model. It can teach concision, source-grounding or response style if pairs represent those qualities. Easy authored defects are a teaching fixture, not evidence of robust general alignment; inspect length shortcuts, domain shifts and quality regressions.

## Limits and common mistakes

The evaluated instruction subset has32 training,8 validation and8 test source contexts. Pretraining overlap is unknown. Repeated views of these test outputs are not a new independent benchmark. Preserve failed and length-limited answers, exact base/tokenizer identity and data provenance when transferring the workflow. The book's larger TinyLlama/CUDA settings require their own supported hardware execution; the small profiles do not certify them.

## Check your understanding

<details>
<summary>At identical policy/reference, why is a log2 loss compatible with learning?</summary>

The current relative margin is zero, but its derivative through trainable policy log probabilities is nonzero. Reference probabilities remain constant.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can positive reference-relative accuracy coexist with poor generated answers?</summary>

Yes. The objective compares two fixed candidate likelihoods relative to a reference. Actual decoding may produce other completions; independent task/human evaluation is still necessary.

</details>
