## Definition and intuition

Topic modeling creates interpretable descriptions of recurring themes in a collection. A clustering-based topic model adds term weighting and evidence inspection to document groups; a group ID alone is not a topic explanation.

“Group 2” tells a reader nothing about the documents. A topic model should supply evidence for a concise interpretation while allowing the reader to inspect exceptions. Its descriptions are an aid to understanding a collection, not a discovery of immutable categories.

## How it works

### Different model families make different assumptions

Probabilistic topic models can represent documents as mixtures of word-distribution topics. BERTopic’s common recipe instead clusters embedding-based document features, then represents each group with weighted terms or another representation model. Do not assume its c-TF-IDF values are normalized topic probabilities, or that every document has a meaningful mixture distribution simply because the word topic is used.

### From groups to class documents

Concatenate or aggregate the text belonging to each fitted group, build a vocabulary/count table, and apply a declared weighting convention. Frequent terms inside a group matter, but terms common throughout the corpus may be less distinctive. Default c-TF-IDF normalizes class counts and applies a corpus-wide frequency weight. Stop-word and n-gram choices change what becomes a candidate description.

### Meaning needs a reader

Topic coherence measures and attractive names can help compare outputs but do not establish that a topic serves a research question. Read representatives, inspect outliers, examine coverage of important minority material, and record what changes under a controlled parameter adjustment. The explorer deliberately retains weak examples instead of deleting them to make its display look cleaner.

## Worked example

Imagine two fitted groups: one about translation, one about speech recognition. The term model occurs in both; translation is concentrated in one. Raw frequency can rank model first in both, while class-based weighting can prefer a more distinctive term. L08 checks the exact weight formula against BERTopic, then refines descriptions without rerunning clustering.

## Implement it

This small mechanism uses Python’s standard library and illustrative values; it does not claim to run a pretrained model. Run `python3 scripts/run-examples.py --id discovery-topic-modeling`. The connected workflow below supplies the actual model, data, and evaluation.

{/* example:discovery-topic-modeling:start */}

```python
groups = {
    0: ["translation model", "translation errors"],
    1: ["speech model", "speech recognition"],
}
for topic, documents in groups.items():
    tokens = " ".join(documents).split()
    print("class document", topic, ":", tokens)
print(
    "Next stage: class-normalized term counts and corpus weighting, not just common-word counts."
)
```

## When to use it

Use topic modeling to make a collection browsable, guide human annotation, or identify recurring issues worth investigating. The research collection project delivers an inspectable report with source IDs, terms, document evidence, and a record of controlled changes.

## Limits and common mistakes

Topic counts and labels depend on representation, corpus selection, reduction, clustering, and human interpretation. A label refinement may make a group easier to read while leaving a bad assignment untouched. The atlas’s small fictional corpus is a teaching workflow, not evidence about scientific literature trends.

## Check your understanding

<details><summary>If a topic name improves, did the document grouping improve?</summary><p>Not necessarily. Representation-only changes keep assignments fixed. Inspect membership before claiming improved discovery.</p></details>

<details><summary>Are c-TF-IDF weights probabilities of a document’s topic?</summary><p>No. They weight vocabulary terms in aggregated class documents under a stated convention; they do not provide that probability interpretation.</p></details>
