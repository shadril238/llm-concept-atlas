**LLM Concept Atlas** is an independent, open-source learning companion to *Hands-On Large Language Models* by Jay Alammar and Maarten Grootendorst. It is not affiliated with the authors, O’Reilly Media, or the publisher. The lessons, diagrams, simulations, and examples on this site are original. The book’s PDF, text, and artwork are not included in this repository.

## What is covered now

The atlas has **104 concept lessons** spanning all 12 chapters, linked through a curated outline of 95 topic entries. This outline is not the book's complete contents. Lessons currently provide introductory explanations and examples of varying depth; missing sections and deeper practical work are tracked in the [book coverage audit](https://github.com/shadril238/llm-concept-atlas/blob/main/docs/audits/book-gap-report.md) and [lesson depth report](https://github.com/shadril238/llm-concept-atlas/blob/main/docs/audits/lesson-depth-report.md).

Start with [how an LLM processes a sentence](/llm-concept-atlas/paths/how-an-llm-processes-a-sentence/), [from semantic search to RAG](/llm-concept-atlas/paths/from-semantic-search-to-rag/), or [the applications-to-fine-tuning route](/llm-concept-atlas/paths/from-applications-to-fine-tuning/). The [concept directory](/llm-concept-atlas/concept-directory/) and [relationship graph](/llm-concept-atlas/atlas/) offer two other ways to find a topic.

Browser experiments label whether their data is calculated, illustrative, or recorded. The tokenization, LoRA, and named-entity-recognition reference lessons now have pinned environments, recorded CPU workflows, detailed explanations, and topic-specific practice. Most other concept-page snippets remain small teaching calculations; some need replacement because they do not demonstrate their topic’s central mechanism. The classification workflow now has evaluated local evidence; the RAG use case still includes a model sketch awaiting pinned clean-run verification. The [foundation labs](/llm-concept-atlas/labs/foundations/) add real generation, representation inspection, learned playlist embeddings, and checked transformer/cache traces. The [first verified examples](/llm-concept-atlas/labs/first-verified-examples/) now provide a locked offline calculation and a pinned CPU embedding workflow with recorded outputs. Later enhancement phases add evaluation and richer explanations to the existing lessons.

The initial audit maps the first edition's sections and embedded concepts to existing or planned destinations. A mapped page, working toy snippet, or reader completion marker does not certify complete teaching coverage. The authors' linked chapter notebooks remain valuable external implementations; their presence does not mean the same workflow has been implemented in this atlas.

## Primary sources and further reading

- [*Hands-On Large Language Models* official notebooks](https://github.com/HandsOnLLM/Hands-On-Large-Language-Models) — the authors’ code organized by book chapter.
- [Attention Is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762) — the original Transformer paper.
- [Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401) — the original RAG paper.
- [Hugging Face Transformers documentation](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/) — tokenizer, model, pipeline, and generation APIs used in examples.
- [Sentence Transformers documentation](https://www.sbert.net/) — sentence embeddings and semantic search used in retrieval examples.

Each concept lesson links the relevant chapter notebook near the end. Most teaching snippets use Python's standard library; the tokenizer and real-model sketches require additional packages and downloads. The linked notebooks have their own model, package, and hardware requirements. Check the associated model cards and licenses before adapting them.

## Contribute a correction or lesson

See the [contribution guide](https://github.com/shadril238/llm-concept-atlas/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md) for the lesson template, source requirements, graph metadata, and checks. Corrections that make an explanation clearer for a beginner are welcome.

The research-summary corpus is original and fictional. No paid hosted-model result is claimed.

Its original MIT tasks, pinned public Q8 GGUF model, actual generated summaries, and finite tool traces retain model failures. Native format constraints do not establish content correctness. No hosted model is called, and independent teaching review remains pending.
