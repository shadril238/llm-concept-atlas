**LLM Concept Atlas** is an independent, open-source learning companion to *Hands-On Large Language Models* by Jay Alammar and Maarten Grootendorst. It is not affiliated with the authors, O’Reilly Media, or the publisher. The lessons, diagrams, simulations, and examples on this site are original. The book’s PDF, text, and artwork are not included in this repository.

## What is covered now

The atlas has **96 full concept lessons** across all 12 chapters. Every entry in the book's 95-topic chapter outline links to a lesson; some closely related entries share a page. Start with [how an LLM processes a sentence](/llm-concept-atlas/paths/how-an-llm-processes-a-sentence/), [from semantic search to RAG](/llm-concept-atlas/paths/from-semantic-search-to-rag/), or [the applications-to-fine-tuning route](/llm-concept-atlas/paths/from-applications-to-fine-tuning/). The [concept directory](/llm-concept-atlas/concept-directory/) and [relationship graph](/llm-concept-atlas/atlas/) offer two other ways to find a topic.

The browser labs and short Python examples are deliberately small and deterministic. They expose a calculation or decision step; they are **not** the output of a trained model. For an implementation with pretrained weights or substantial training data, follow the authors' chapter notebooks linked from each lesson. The examples in this atlas explain the mechanism before that larger run.

## Primary sources and further reading

- [*Hands-On Large Language Models* official notebooks](https://github.com/HandsOnLLM/Hands-On-Large-Language-Models) — the authors’ code organized by book chapter.
- [Attention Is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762) — the original Transformer paper.
- [Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401) — the original RAG paper.
- [Hugging Face Transformers documentation](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/) — tokenizer, model, pipeline, and generation APIs used in examples.
- [Sentence Transformers documentation](https://www.sbert.net/) — sentence embeddings and semantic search used in retrieval examples.

Each concept lesson links the relevant chapter notebook near the end. The site's teaching snippets use Python's standard library; the linked notebooks have their own package, model, and hardware requirements. Check a model's license and model card before adapting a notebook to your own use.
